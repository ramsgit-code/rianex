"We have to implement AI" has become a management committee phrase. The problem is that it almost never comes with a goal behind it, and an implementation without a goal ends the same way in every company: ten ChatGPT licences, two people who really use it and nobody able to say what has changed in the income statement.

This article is the plan we follow with the companies that call us about this. It is written for whoever decides, not for whoever programs.

## The short answer

Implementing AI in a company means choosing a process that costs money, measuring it, connecting an AI model to the tools where that process already happens and leaving a person reviewing what the AI decides. It is done in five steps: choose where, put the data in order, decide whether to buy or build, get the team to use it and comply with the law. A well-scoped first project is in production in four to eight weeks.

If you already know which process you want to automate, jump to [AI process automation: where to start](/en/blog/automatizar-procesos-con-ia-por-donde-empezar), which goes into detail on how to choose and measure that first process.

## Step 1: choose where, starting with the money

AI is not implemented in a company. It is implemented in a process. And the first one should be as close as possible to where the money comes in: lead capture, replying to contacts, proposals, sales follow-up.

There are two reasons. The first is that there the return is measured in sales and not in hours, and a sale that used to be lost convinces any committee. The second is that those processes usually have a clear bottleneck: a contact who waits a day for a reply, a proposal that takes three to go out.

The questions that help you choose:

- Which task repeats every week and is always done by the same person?
- Where are opportunities lost because of slow replies?
- Which work requires reading or writing text, and not just moving data from one place to another?

If the answer to the third is "none", you probably do not need AI yet: you need a rule-based automation, which is cheaper and more predictable.

## Step 2: put the data in order before connecting anything

An AI model is as good as the information it has access to. If the contacts are spread across the email of three salespeople, a spreadsheet and the manager's WhatsApp, the AI will be working blind.

You do not need a six-month data project. You need the chosen process to have **one place where each piece of data lives**: the contacts in the CRM, the appointments in the calendar, the prices in one place and not in someone's head. If you have no CRM, setting it up is usually the first deliverable of the project, not a separate step. We cover it in [what GoHighLevel is and when it pays off](/en/blog/que-es-gohighlevel).

Before touching anything, measure the process as it is today: how long it takes, how many times it happens per month and how much is lost along the way. Without that initial snapshot you will not be able to show that the AI has changed anything. The method is in [how to measure a process before automating it](/en/blog/medir-un-proceso-antes-de-automatizarlo).

## Step 3: buy, configure or build

There are three ways to bring AI into a process, and it is best to go from the cheapest to the most expensive:

1. **Use what you already pay for.** Many CRMs and office tools [already come with AI features](https://www.hubspot.com/products/artificial-intelligence "follow"). If the process is not what sets you apart, configure them and stop there.
2. **Connect tools with automations.** Platforms such as n8n or Make connect your CRM, your email and an AI model without custom development. They cover most of an SME's processes. We compare them in [AI for automating processes: which tools to use](/en/blog/herramientas-ia-para-automatizar-procesos).
3. **Build to measure.** When the process has many exceptions, touches several systems or is exactly what makes you different from the competition. Examples, timelines and price in [custom AI projects](/en/blog/proyectos-de-ia-a-medida-ejemplos-plazos-precio).

You almost never need to train your own model. What gets built is everything around the model: where it gets the information from, which rules it has to follow, where it writes the result and who reviews it.

## Step 4: get the team to use it

This is the step that sinks the most projects, and there is nothing technical about it. A system the team does not use has implemented nothing.

What works:

- **The AI should remove work, not add it.** If using it means opening another screen and copying data, it will not be used. It has to work inside the tools where the team already works.
- **A person should review what the AI decides**, above all at the beginning. The review gives the team confidence and protects you from expensive mistakes. Over time, what the AI always gets right can move to running by itself.
- **Someone should be responsible for the system** inside the company: the person who raises the alarm when something fails and who decides what gets improved.
- **Short, practical training**, on the real process and not on AI in general.

## Step 5: comply with the law from the start

Two regulations apply to almost any implementation in Spain:

- **The GDPR**, as soon as the AI processes personal data: you need to know where it is processed, [have a data processing agreement with each provider](https://www.aepd.es/preguntas-frecuentes/2-tus-obligaciones-como-responsable-del-tratamiento/8-responsable-y-encargado-del-tratamiento/FAQ-0238-cual-seria-el-contenido-del-contrato-de-encargo-de-tratamiento "follow") and inform the people concerned. With health or financial data, your own server in the European Union simplifies things a lot. We explain it in [GDPR and automation with AI](/en/blog/rgpd-y-automatizacion-con-ia).
- **The EU AI Act**, which [requires that people know they are talking to an AI](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=DOUE-L-2024-81079 "follow") (for example, with a WhatsApp agent) and that whoever uses these systems in the company [has sufficient training](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers "follow").

Neither of the two prevents you from implementing AI. What they ask is that you do it knowing which data each system uses and who answers for it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I start implementing AI in my company?

With a specific process that costs money, if possible close to sales: replying to contacts, proposals or sales follow-up. Measure it before touching it and build only for that process. The rest of the company comes later, with a result to show.

### Do I need a technical team to implement AI?

Not to start. You need someone inside the company who is responsible for the process and the system, and someone who builds and maintains it, who can be external. What is advisable is for the code and the infrastructure to be in the company's name.

### Does the data have to be perfect?

No. The process you are going to automate needs to have each piece of data in one place: the contacts in the CRM, the appointments in the calendar. Putting that in order is usually part of the first project.

### Is AI going to replace my team?

In the projects that work, no. It takes the repetitive part of the work away from people and leaves them the part that requires judgement. Projects that try to remove human judgement from a process that needs it end up with someone reviewing by hand everything the machine does.
