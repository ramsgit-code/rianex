The question that comes up most in a first call is not how much it costs or how long it takes. It is a more basic one: "fine, but in my company, what exactly can be automated?".

It is a good question, because almost everything published about AI automation speaks in the abstract. Efficiency, productivity, transformation. Nothing you can put on Monday's agenda.

So here are fifteen specific processes, grouped by department, as they appear in service SMEs. In each one we separate two things that should not be mixed: what the automation does, which is moving data and triggering actions according to rules, and where AI really comes in, which is where judgement is needed.

## The short answer

The processes that are best automated with AI in an SME are the ones that repeat every day, follow rules that can be explained and receive untidy information: messages, emails, forms with free text or documents. The automation moves that information and triggers the actions. The AI reads it, classifies it and writes the reply.

Three of the fifteen are new in this November 2026 revision, because there are already companies running them in production: giving a fixed quote in one minute, serving your customers' AI agents and reviewing accounting documents before a person signs them off.

If a process does not repeat, has no clear rules or has no cost that can be measured, it is not ready to be automated yet. ## Sales and lead generation

This is the department where the return comes soonest, because every failure in the process is a sale that does not happen.

### 1. Reply to every enquiry in under a minute

**What is automated.** Any request that comes in by form, WhatsApp, email or missed call is logged in the CRM and gets an immediate reply with the next step: a question, a booking link or a note saying when they will be called.

**Where AI comes in.** In reading the message. "Hi, I wanted to know prices for 40 people in March" is not the same as "do you work on Saturdays?". The AI understands what each person is asking and picks the reply, instead of sending everyone the same text.

### 2. Qualify before the first call

**What is automated.** A few qualifying questions before anyone on the team steps in: what they need, how urgently, what budget they have. With the answers, the contact is scored and assigned to the right person.

**Where AI comes in.** When the answers are free text. A model can read "we need something for next week, we are a consultancy of 15 people" and extract urgency and size without forcing anyone to fill in ten boxes. We cover this in [how to qualify leads automatically](/en/blog/como-cualificar-leads-automaticamente).

### 3. Generate sales proposals

**What is automated.** After the call, a short form captures what was discussed and the proposal is generated on its own with the template, the prices and the terms, ready to review and send. The follow-up reminders are scheduled.

**Where AI comes in.** In writing the parts that change from one client to the next: the summary of what they need, the reasoning behind the recommended option. The rest is rules.

This is what we built at [Eventos Barcelona](/en/casos-de-exito/eventos-barcelona): each proposal took between one and three days to go out and now goes out in minutes, with 85% less response time to the client.

### 4. Follow up on opportunities that go cold

**What is automated.** If a quote has gone seven days without a reply, a reminder to the salesperson or a message to the client. If it has gone thirty, it moves to a reactivation sequence. Nobody has to remember.

**Where AI comes in.** In personalising the message with what was discussed in the call, so it does not sound like a template. And in detecting, when the client replies, whether it is a yes, a no or a "not now".

### 5. Give a fixed quote in one minute

**What is automated.** A short form (what they need, how much, by when) calculates the price from the company's rates and sends it straight away, fixed, with the link to book or sign. Whatever does not fit the rules goes to a person with the data already collected.

**Where AI comes in.** In reading what the client writes in their own words and in detecting the cases that fall outside the rate card. The price is calculated by the rules, not by the model: a made-up price is worse than giving none.

This month's reference is Alecto, a British firm that handles home sales and purchases with AI and qualified lawyers. Its website promises a fixed quote "in under a minute" and that you pay what was quoted even if the case gets complicated ([alectolegal.com](https://www.alectolegal.com)). For an SME with repeatable services, an immediate indicative price removes the first point of friction.

## Customer service

### 6. Answer frequent questions on WhatsApp and the website

**What is automated.** The questions that arrive a hundred times a month (opening hours, indicative prices, lead times, how it works) are answered on their own, at any hour. Whatever does not fit goes to a person with the full conversation.

**Where AI comes in.** Here AI is the core: an [AI agent](/en/blog/agentes-de-ia-para-empresas-cuando-compensan) trained on the company's real information understands the question even if it is badly written and answers with correct data. The condition is that it knows when it does not know and hands over.

### 7. Classify and route the inbox

**What is automated.** Incoming emails and messages are labelled (sales enquiry, incident, invoice, supplier, spam) and reach the right person or queue.

**Where AI comes in.** In the classification. A keyword rule fails as soon as someone writes differently; a model understands the intent of the message. It is one of the AI applications with the best ratio of effort to result.

### 8. Confirm appointments and reduce no-shows

**What is automated.** A reminder on booking, another before the appointment and another on the day, with the option to confirm or change. The reply updates the calendar without anyone typing it in, and if someone cancels, whoever was waiting for a slot is notified.

**Where AI comes in.** Very little, and that is fine. It is only needed when the client replies in free text ("Thursday afternoon would be better if possible") and it has to be understood. We look at it in detail in [automation for clinics](/en/blog/automatizacion-para-clinicas-captacion-y-agenda).

### 9. Serve your customers' AI agents

**What is automated.** More and more requests are not made by a person but by an AI assistant on their behalf: it calls to book an appointment, asks for a price or tries to buy. What gets automated is the reply and, above all, the rules: what an agent can look up (opening hours, availability, prices), what it can book on its own, up to what amount it can pay and from what point a person is needed.

**Where AI comes in.** In the business's WhatsApp or voice agent, which understands the request whoever it comes from. The rest is having the information clear and organised on the website, because that is the first thing those assistants read.

It is not science fiction: in the US, Google's free agent already calls businesses on the user's behalf, and Walmart has announced it will let its customers' agents buy with a spending limit set by the customer ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/21/walmart-amazon-agents-muse)). It has not reached Spain yet, but the design (what is done on its own, what asks for permission) is the same one any agent should already have.

## Operations and administration

### 10. Read invoices, orders and delivery notes

**What is automated.** The documents that arrive by email are saved, logged and passed to the accounting system or the ERP, with their supplier, date and amount.

**Where AI comes in.** In extracting the data from documents that have no fixed format. Every supplier lays out its invoice in its own way, and a model can read them all without a template per supplier. It is worth keeping a human review for amounts above a threshold.

### 11. Move data between tools that do not talk to each other

**What is automated.** The client closed in the CRM appears in the invoicing tool, in the project tool and in the tracking sheet, with no copy and paste. It is the least eye-catching part and the one that saves the most hours in almost every company.

**Where AI comes in.** Usually nowhere. It is pure integration: if a piece of data can be moved with a rule, it should be moved with a rule. We explain it in [integrating GoHighLevel with your stack](/en/blog/integrar-gohighlevel-con-tu-stack).

### 12. Meeting summaries and tasks

**What is automated.** When a call with a client ends, the transcript is saved in their record, the summary goes to the team and the agreed tasks are created with an owner and a date.

**Where AI comes in.** In the whole process: transcribing, summarising and extracting who committed to what. It is one of the cases where AI does a job that simply was not done before.

### 13. Review accounting documents before a person signs them off

**What is automated.** The client's documents (invoices, statements, filed tax forms) are collected in one place, cross-checked, and anything that does not match or is missing is flagged, before the professional reviews them.

**Where AI comes in.** In reading documents in different formats and in asking the questions: this invoice does not appear in the bank, this expense does not fit with the previous quarter's. The signature still belongs to a person.

The reference is a trial by KPMG in the UK: more than a hundred agents worked alongside the audit team on the accounts of a listed company, in several cases they went deeper than the team and they raised questions the auditors had not asked themselves. But it was not an official audit, an auditor had to correct an agent that did not cross-check the regulations properly against the company's documents, and the big obstacle was untidy data ([European Business Magazine](https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/ai-agents-better-questions-kpmg/)). In an accounting firm the same thing happens on a small scale: first you sort out where the data comes in from.

## Management and marketing

### 14. Reports that prepare themselves

**What is automated.** The week's numbers (contacts, sales, appointments, campaigns) are collected from each tool and sent in a report to whoever needs to see them, on the same day at the same time.

**Where AI comes in.** In the reading: a paragraph that says what has changed compared with the previous week and what deserves attention. The numbers are calculated by a query, not by a model, so that it does not make them up.

### 15. Reactivate old customers

**What is automated.** Customers who bought six or twelve months ago and have not come back get a message at the right moment: a check-up, a renewal, a piece of news that affects them.

**Where AI comes in.** In choosing the message according to what each one bought and in understanding the replies. It is the cheapest thing there is in lead generation and almost nobody does it consistently.

## What should not be automated yet

Not everything that can be automated should be automated. There are three situations in which we recommend waiting.

**Processes that change every time.** If every proposal, every quote or every order is resolved according to the judgement of the day, the judgement has to be defined first. Automating before that is programming improvisation.

**Decisions with serious consequences and no review.** Approving a loan, giving a diagnosis, answering a legal claim, signing an audit or a contract. AI can prepare the work; the decision is signed by a person. With sensitive data there are also legal limits, which we go through in [GDPR and AI automation](/en/blog/rgpd-y-automatizacion-con-ia).

**Processes that happen rarely.** Something that happens twice a month rarely pays for what it costs to build and maintain. A good template is usually enough there.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between automating a process and automating it with AI?

Automation executes rules: if this happens, do that. AI is added when the input is free text or documents with no fixed format and they need to be interpreted. Most projects are mainly automation, with AI only at the points a rule cannot reach.

### Do I need to change tools to automate?

Not always. Many processes are automated by connecting what is already in use. When contacts live in several inboxes or in spreadsheets, it usually does pay to centralise them in a CRM first, because without a single place there is nothing to automate reliably.

### How long does it take to automate the first process?

A well-scoped first process is usually implemented in four to six weeks, including the phase of defining how it should work, which is the one that drags on the most.

### Can an AI agent call my business?

It already happens in the US: Google's free assistant calls businesses on the user's behalf to ask a question or book an appointment. It is not available in Spain yet, but it is worth getting ready anyway: always answer, with a person or with an agent, have clear services and prices on the website and decide what can be booked or paid for without human intervention.
