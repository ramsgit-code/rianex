WhatsApp is the channel where an SME's customers already are, reply and read. That is why it is tempting to use it to sell more to people who have already bought. And for the same reason it is the channel where it is easiest to ruin things: a message that is out of place does not end up in the promotions folder, it ends up in a block.

Cross-selling on WhatsApp works when it resembles a good shop assistant and not a campaign. This article explains how to automate it so that it is the former.

## The short answer

Cross-selling on WhatsApp that works is small, timely and related: one message to a customer who has already bought, at the moment when the complementary product solves something for them, with a single offer and an easy way out. It is automated with the WhatsApp Business API connected to the CRM: the CRM decides who and when based on what they bought, WhatsApp sends it with a template approved by Meta and any reply goes to a person or to an assistant with clear instructions. Without the customer's consent to receive commercial messages on WhatsApp, nothing is sent.

## Why WhatsApp burns out faster than email

A badly conceived commercial email gets ignored. A badly conceived commercial WhatsApp has three consequences that email does not.

**The customer blocks or reports the number.** WhatsApp takes it into account: Meta assigns a [quality rating to each business number](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messages/send-messages "follow") and, if blocks and reports go up, it limits how many messages the number can send or pauses templates. A bad mass send also affects service messages, the ones that confirm appointments or give notice of an order.

**Every commercial template costs money.** Messages the company starts outside an open conversation are sent with templates, and [Meta charges for each marketing message delivered](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing "follow"). A send to the whole database is not free even if it looks like it.

**The channel is personal.** The same customer who appreciates an appointment reminder on WhatsApp sees a generic offer in the same chat as an intrusion. Tolerance is much lower than with email.

None of the three things prevents cross-selling on WhatsApp. They force you to do it well.

## The four conditions of a good cross-sell message

**Related to what they bought.** The offer has to be the logical continuation of the previous purchase: maintenance after the installation, the second session of the treatment, insurance after the trip, the advanced course after the basic one. If you have to explain why they are being offered it, it is not related.

**At the moment they need it.** The best moment is not right after paying, when the customer has not yet tried what they bought. It is when the complementary product solves something that has just come up: when the consumable runs out, when the first phase ends, when the date on which the first product stops being useful is approaching.

**One thing only.** One message, one offer, one step. A catalogue on WhatsApp does not get read.

**With an easy way out.** The customer should be able to say "not now" or "don't write to me again" with a button, and the system should really respect it. It is what keeps the number's quality rating high, and what the law requires.

## How to automate it step by step

### 1. Centralise purchases and conversations in the CRM

Automatic cross-selling needs to know what each customer bought and when. If sales live in the invoicing program and conversations on each salesperson's phone, there is nothing to automate. The first step is for purchases, conversations and consents to be in the same place. We explain it in [WhatsApp for sales: how to set up a real system](/en/blog/whatsapp-automatizacion-ventas) and in [integrating GoHighLevel with your stack](/en/blog/integrar-gohighlevel-con-tu-stack).

### 2. Define the triggers

Each cross-sell is defined as a rule of the type "if they bought X and N days have passed and they have not bought Y, send template Z". The triggers come from the business, not from the tool: ask your best salesperson what they offer to whom and at what moment, and turn it into rules. Three or four well-chosen triggers perform better than twenty.

### 3. Create the templates and get Meta to approve them

Messages the company starts are sent with [templates that Meta reviews before allowing their use, and that it classifies as marketing, utility or authentication](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/overview "follow"). An offer is marketing even if it is disguised as something else; trying to slip it through as utility usually ends in rejection or reclassification. Templates accept variables (the name, the product they bought, the date) and quick-reply buttons, which are the most convenient way to give the way out.

### 4. Decide what happens when the customer replies

When the customer replies, a 24-hour window opens in which the company can write to them with free-form messages, without a template. There are two options there: the reply reaches a member of the team with all the context in the CRM, or it is handled by an AI assistant that knows the product, answers questions and hands over to a person as soon as there is buying intent or something goes off script. For small volumes, the first is enough.

### 5. Respect opt-outs and limits

If the customer presses "not interested", they do not get that offer again. If they press "I don't want commercial messages", they stop receiving marketing on WhatsApp, although they keep receiving service messages. And it is worth setting a cap: no more than one commercial message per customer every so often, decided by the business and enforced by the CRM, not by anyone's memory.

## Where AI comes in and where it does not

The decision about who to write to and when does not need AI. It is a rule on purchase data, and a rule is cheaper, more predictable and easier to audit.

AI adds value in two places. The first is in the replies: customers answer in free text ("and does that work for the old model?") and a well-instructed assistant can settle the question on the spot. The second is in detecting opportunities that are not in the rules: reading service conversations and flagging the customer who has just described a problem that another product solves. That flag goes to a person, not to an automatic send.

What is not a good idea is letting a model freely write offers that are sent without review. The price, the terms and the promise have to come from data and rules.

## What the law says

In Spain, sending commercial communications by electronic means requires, as a general rule, the prior consent of the recipient ([article 21 of the LSSI](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758#a21 "follow")). There is an exception for customers with whom a contractual relationship already exists, when the offer is for products or services similar to those they bought and they are offered the chance to unsubscribe in every message.

In addition, WhatsApp [requires on its own account that the customer has agreed](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/getting-opt-in "follow") to receive messages from the company on WhatsApp before the company writes to them. It is a condition of the platform, independent of what the law allows.

In practice: ask for permission explicitly when you register the customer or in the first conversation, store it in the CRM with the date and the channel, and do not send anything commercial to anyone who does not have it. This is not legal advice; your adviser should review the specific case. We go further in [GDPR and automation with AI](/en/blog/rgpd-y-automatizacion-con-ia).

## What to measure

Four numbers are enough to know whether cross-selling adds or subtracts:

- **Reply rate per trigger.** What percentage of customers reply to each template. If a trigger gets hardly any replies, the moment or the offer is badly chosen.
- **Conversion to purchase.** How many buy the product offered within a reasonable period after the message.
- **Opt-outs and blocks.** How many ask not to receive more messages. If they go up, you stop and review before carrying on.
- **Cost per sale.** What the marketing messages sent cost divided by the sales they generate.

The third is the one that protects the channel. A campaign that sells but sends opt-outs soaring is spending trust that cannot be recovered afterwards.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you cross-sell with the normal WhatsApp Business app?

You can by hand, customer by customer. To automate it with triggers, templates, buttons and logging in the CRM you need the WhatsApp Business API, connected directly or through a provider or a CRM with a native integration such as GoHighLevel.

### How much does it cost to send cross-sell messages on WhatsApp?

Meta charges for each marketing template message delivered, at a rate that depends on the recipient's country. Replies within a conversation opened by the customer are not charged as marketing, although since October 2026 Meta also charges service messages above a certain monthly volume. Check Meta's current price list before calculating, because it changes.

### Do I need the customer's consent to write to them on WhatsApp?

Yes. WhatsApp requires the customer to have agreed to receive messages from the company on that channel, and Spanish law requires consent for commercial communications except for the exception of customers with similar products. Store the consent in the CRM with its date.

### How often can I send offers on WhatsApp?

There is no universal figure, but there is a criterion: the fewer the messages and the more related to what they bought, the better. Set a cap per customer in the CRM and watch the opt-outs; if they go up, you are sending too much.
