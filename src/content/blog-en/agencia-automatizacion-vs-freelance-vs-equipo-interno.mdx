When a company decides to automate processes with AI, the first question is not which tool to use, but who is going to do it. There are three routes: hiring an agency, hiring a freelancer or having someone on the payroll. All three work and all three fail, depending on the case.

Disclosure of interest: we are an agency. That is why this comparison also says when an agency is not right for you, and the cost data comes from external sources, not from our prices.

## The short answer

- **Freelancer**, for a scoped and clear project (connecting two tools, building one specific workflow) when you have someone in-house who knows how to brief and review.
- **Agency**, to automate several processes with a measurable result, when you have no technical profile in-house and you need someone to answer for the system after delivering it.
- **In-house team**, when automation is continuous and strategic: many processes, changes every week and a volume that justifies a fixed salary.

The usual pattern in an SME is to start with an agency or a freelancer and, once there is volume, hire someone to maintain it with external support.

## Freelancer: fast and cheap if the brief is clear

**When it works.** The brief fits in one sentence ("when a lead comes in through the form, create it in HubSpot and send a WhatsApp") and someone in your company knows how to check that it is done properly. For that, a good freelancer is the cheapest and fastest option.

**Risks.**
- If that person stops being available, nobody else knows the system.
- Maintenance and documentation are not usually in the price: when an API changes, the workflow breaks and you have to hire again.
- Nobody checks whether the process you asked for was the one that needed automating. The freelancer does what you ask.

## Agency: from the assessment to the result

**When it works.** You want a business result (replying to every lead within minutes, removing hours of paperwork) and you have nobody in-house who knows how to translate it into workflows and tools. An agency does the assessment, designs, builds, trains the team and answers for the system by contract.

**Risks.**
- It is more expensive per hour than a freelancer.
- Some set everything up in their own accounts and tools: if you leave, you are left without the system. Require by contract that the credentials, the workflows and the code are in your company's name.
- If the brief is just "connect A to B", you are paying for structure you do not need.

## In-house team: when automation is the day-to-day

**When it works.** There are many processes to automate, they change often and the workload is enough for one full-time person. Someone on the inside knows the business better than anyone and does not need the context explained every time.

**Risks.**
- It is a fixed cost: salary plus social security contributions, including the months with little work.
- Hiring an AI automation profile is slow and expensive, and it is a profile with high turnover.
- A single person in-house has the same problem as a freelancer: if they leave, they take the knowledge with them. Documenting is mandatory.

## How to decide: four questions

1. **Does the brief fit in one sentence?** Yes: freelancer. No, because the process has to be understood first: agency or in-house.
2. **Who is going to maintain it a year from now?** If the answer is "nobody", you need contracted maintenance or an in-house profile.
3. **How many processes are on the list?** One or two: freelancer or agency. Ten and growing: think about someone on the payroll.
4. **Do you have someone who knows how to brief and review?** If not, the risk of a freelancer goes up a lot.

Whichever route you take, there are three things you should always require:
- That everything is in your company's name: accounts, credentials, workflows and code.
- That the system is documented.
- That the baseline is measured before starting, to know whether it has worked (we explain it in [measuring a process before automating it](/en/blog/medir-un-proceso-antes-de-automatizarlo)).

## When an agency is not right for you

If the brief is small and clear, if you already have someone technical in-house who only needs a hand now and then, or if automation is your product and not an improvement to your processes. In those cases, a freelancer or hiring is a better use of the money.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does an automation freelancer charge in Spain?

There is no official statistic. The 2026 references put a mid-level tech profile between €40 and €70/hour and a senior one between €70 and €120/hour. For n8n or Make specifically we have not found reliable data in Spain.

### How much does it cost to have an AI person on the payroll?

According to 2026 aggregators, an AI engineer in Spain has a median gross salary of about €66,900 a year, and a mid-level profile in a Spanish company €42,000 to €55,000. To that you have to add the employer's social security contributions.

### What is best for an SME?

For most SMEs that are starting out, an agency or a freelancer, depending on whether the brief is clear or the process has to be understood first. An in-house profile pays off when there is a continuous volume of processes to automate and maintain.

### How do I avoid being tied to an agency or a freelancer?

Require by contract that the accounts, credentials, workflows and code are in your company's name, that the system is documented and that you can take it to another provider with no exit cost.
